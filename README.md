💬 Interview Questions & Technical Responses
1. What is the importance of data visualization?
It translates complex, raw data tables into clear visual patterns. This allows stakeholders to instantly spot trends, outliers, and critical business problems that would otherwise remain hidden in rows of text.
2. When do you use a pie chart vs bar chart?
Use a pie chart only when comparing parts of a whole with a very small number of categories (2–3 slices maximum, like gender or yes/no choices). Use a bar chart when comparing values across larger categories (like regions or product sub-categories) because the human eye is significantly better at comparing straight lengths than angles.
3. How do you make visualizations more engaging?
By adding clean interactive filters, building sequential data stories, keeping the design free of clutter, and using color purposefully (such as a diverging color scale to instantly highlight financial losses).
4. What is data storytelling?
Data storytelling is the practice of combining data visualizations with a structured narrative context. Instead of just showing what the numbers are, it explains why they matter and recommends specific business actions.
5. How do you avoid misleading visualizations?
Always start the numerical axes at zero to keep proportions honest, maintain consistent time intervals on line scales, and avoid heavy visual distractions like 3D charts or distorted sizing.
6. What are best practices in dashboard design?
Organize content hierarchically (most important KPIs at the top left).
Limit the dashboard to 3 or 4 relevant charts to prevent clutter.
Use a single, functional color palette.
Keep formatting clean by hiding unnecessary or redundant technical field labels.
7. What tools have you used for visualization?
I utilize Tableau Public for building highly interactive dashboards, managing sheets, setting up custom actions, and publishing cloud-hosted visual data stories.
